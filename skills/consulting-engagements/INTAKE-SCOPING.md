# Intake & Scoping

Win and bound the work: qualify the opportunity, run discovery, capture decision-relevant context, convert ambiguity into a scoped proposal/SOW, and kick off cleanly. **Never produce a proposal or plan before discovery is sufficient.**

## 1. Opportunity qualification (before scoping)

Score an inbound lead before investing in a proposal. Pursue / nurture / decline.

| Check | Pursue signal | Red flag |
|------|---------------|----------|
| Problem clarity | Specific, owned, urgent | "Can you help with strategy?" |
| Decision authority | Talking to the buyer/sponsor | No access to decision-maker |
| Budget signal | Range or process exists | "What would you charge?" with no frame |
| Urgency / trigger | Real event forcing action | "Sometime this year" |
| Delivery risk | Scope you can deliver well | Outside competence, politically toxic |

Output: fit score, red flags, missing info, recommended next step, discovery-call question set. **Do not recommend acceptance when scope, authority, or expectations are unclear.**

## 2. Discovery call structure

**Objective:** uncover business context, the real decision, constraints, stakeholders, success metrics, urgency — not symptoms.

Question themes (pull 3-5 per theme):

- **Context** — what's happening, what changed, what triggered this now?
- **Problem** — what's the cost of inaction? What's been tried? Why did it fail?
- **Decision** — what decision does this support, who makes it, by when?
- **Success** — what does "good" look like, measurably? Who judges?
- **Stakeholders** — sponsor, decision-maker, influencers, blockers, users, approvers.
- **Constraints** — budget, timeline, politics, prior commitments, data access.
- **Scope** — what's explicitly in and out? What assumptions are we making?

**Discipline:** do not diagnose before evidence. Capture symptoms separately from root cause. Flag any topic needing legal / financial / HR / technical specialist review.

## 3. Intake form (capture, then synthesize to a brief)

| Section | Fields |
|---------|--------|
| Client basics | Org, contact, role, sector, geography, website |
| Business context | Current situation, trigger, strategic priority, urgency |
| Problem statement | What's happening, why it matters, what's been tried |
| Decision to support | What decision, by whom, by when |
| Objectives | Outcomes, success criteria, measurable indicators |
| Stakeholders | Sponsor, decision-maker, influencers, blockers, users, approvers |
| Scope | In / out, known constraints, assumptions |
| Deliverables | Format, audience, level of detail, deadline |
| Data / materials | Available docs, systems, interviews, quality, access limits |
| Commercials | Budget range, pricing preference, procurement, invoicing |
| Risk | Legal, financial, HR, regulatory, confidentiality, reputational, political |
| Working style | Cadence, communication, tone, review process |
| AI / data permissions | Whether AI use is allowed, data restrictions, redaction needs |

## 4. Intake completeness check (gate before proposing or researching)

Compare context against the intake standard. Rank gaps by **risk to delivery**, not count.

- **Critical gap** — blocks safe scoping/delivery (no decision defined, no success metric, no sponsor, unknown budget order-of-magnitude). → **Must resolve before proceeding.**
- **Nice-to-have gap** — refine later (exact stakeholder titles, secondary metrics). → proceed, note as assumption.

Output: completeness score, ranked missing inputs, clarifying questions, **"safe to proceed?"** recommendation. Suggest a low-risk assumption only where a gap is non-critical; label it.

## 5. Proposal / SOW

Convert discovery into a bounded commercial offer. Structure:

1. **Executive summary** — the client's words for their problem and desired outcome.
2. **Understanding of need** — proves you listened; states the decision being supported.
3. **Proposed approach** — phases tied to outcomes, not activity for its own sake.
4. **Deliverables** — concrete, with acceptance criteria (what each will and will not include).
5. **Timeline & milestones** — realistic, with client dependencies named.
6. **Responsibilities** — what you own, what the client owes, and when.
7. **Assumptions** — every premise the price and timeline depend on.
8. **Exclusions** — explicitly out of scope; the scope-creep firewall.
9. **Pricing & terms** — plain-language commercial terms (not legal contract language).
10. **Next steps** — the single clear action.

**Guardrails:** do not write legal contract language as legal advice — plain commercial terms for attorney/client review. Vague deliverables, hidden assumptions, and missing acceptance criteria are the top failure modes. Run the scope-risk check (§6) before sending.

## 6. Pricing, effort & scope-risk check

Pressure-test before sending a proposal or accepting a change.

- Break work into tasks; estimate effort in bands (not false precision).
- Count the work consultants under-price: meetings, revisions, synthesis time, stakeholder management, client delays.
- Identify dependencies and what happens when the client is late.
- Flag margin risk and complexity; recommend scope or pricing adjustments.

Be conservative and transparent. Separate the estimate from final consultant judgment — **no guarantee of profitability.**

## 7. Scope-creep control

1. Maintain explicit in-scope / out-of-scope lists.
2. Log every new request in a scope-change log.
3. Label each new ask: included · minor clarification · paid add-on · out of scope.
4. Re-run the proposal/scope-risk check before accepting changes.
5. **Never let an AI-drafted message commit to additional work without consultant approval.**

## 8. Engagement kickoff & workplan

After approval, before delivery: kickoff agenda, workplan with phases, milestone tracker, RACI-lite, communication cadence, decision process, escalation path, risk register. Do not modify contractual scope without explicit approval; flag any SOW-vs-expectation mismatch.
