# Synthesis & Deliverables

Turn evidence into structured thinking, then into client-ready artifacts. Every deliverable answers **what · so what · now what** and ties back to the client's decision.

## 1. Issue tree & hypotheses (structure before research or production)

Break the client's decision into drivers, then hypotheses, then the evidence each needs.

- Start from **the decision**, not the topic.
- Branches should be distinct and collectively cover the decision (MECE in spirit, not academic purity).
- Attach a hypothesis and the evidence that would confirm/refute it to each branch.
- Prioritize the branches that most change the recommendation.

Failure modes: academic frameworks with no client relevance, false MECE, too many branches, analysis paralysis. Keep it tied to scope.

## 2. Strategy options & recommendation

Convert analysis into a decision, not a menu.

1. Define the decision criteria (from the client's success metrics).
2. Generate genuinely distinct options — different bets, not flavors of one.
3. Evaluate tradeoffs against the criteria.
4. Stress-test assumptions behind each.
5. **Recommend a path** — opinionated but transparent: state why the alternatives lost.
6. State the conditions under which the recommendation would change.

| Option | Description | Pros | Cons | Key risks | Fit to criteria |
|--------|-------------|------|------|-----------|-----------------|

Failure modes: bland "consider all options," no prioritization, hidden assumptions, a recommendation not traceable to evidence. Do not present speculative strategy as certainty.

## 3. Bias & overconfidence controls (apply to any diagnosis)

- **Confidence labels** — High / Medium / Low on major claims, by evidence quality.
- **Alternative explanation** — at least one plausible competing read for every major diagnosis.
- **Assumption register** — list assumptions explicitly with a validation step for each.
- **Stakeholder perspective check** — how different stakeholders will read the recommendation.
- **Bias scan** — check for stereotyping, unsupported demographic assumptions, one-sided framing.

## 4. Decision memo (answer-first)

For board / sponsor / CEO decision points. One page.

1. **The decision** being requested (the ask, up top).
2. **Recommendation** — your answer first.
3. **Why** — the 2-4 evidence points that carry it.
4. **Options considered** — briefly, with why not.
5. **Risks & assumptions** — including what's unverified.
6. **Next steps** — owners and dates.

Failure modes: too much background, no clear ask, buried recommendation, weak "so what." Do not hide uncertainty — label assumptions and open questions.

## 5. Executive brief / report

Polished written deliverable from research/analysis.

- Confirm objective and audience → structure outline → draft → integrate **sourced** evidence → tighten → QA.
- Structured, concise, evidence-aware, action-oriented, written for business readers.
- Clear hierarchy; recommendation present; consistent terminology.
- All external facts carry source support or qualification (see [RESEARCH-EVIDENCE.md](RESEARCH-EVIDENCE.md)).

## 6. Client-ready deck

Translate analysis into an executive narrative.

- Build the **storyline first** (what → so what → now what), then sequence slides.
- **Every slide has a message, not a topic.** The slide title states the takeaway, not the subject.
- Recommend visuals; **mark placeholders for any chart or number** — never invent data or fabricate a chart.
- End on a decision ask.

Failure modes: slide bloat, descriptive (topic) titles, weak flow, too many frameworks, no ask.

## 7. Artifacts & templates

Reusable frameworks, worksheets, canvases, trackers, SOPs, workshop tools.

- Define the user and the job → required fields → draft → test against a scenario → simplify → add instructions.
- Usable without explanation, lightweight, fit to the client context.
- Avoid compliance / HR / legal / financial templates that imply professional certification unless reviewed.

## 8. Style, voice & executive clarity (last pass before QA)

- Preserve meaning; tighten wording; improve structure; simplify jargon; align tone; check consistency.
- **Improve clarity without changing substance or adding new claims.**
- Do not change recommendations or evidence while editing — if a change is needed, flag it, don't silently make it.
