# Research Appendix

This appendix records the evidence base used to design the skill, not sources for any future book. Sources were read and synthesized from live public pages available on 2026-07-16. Most are normative standards, acquisition guidance, or expert craft practice; they do not empirically prove that one workflow will produce a successful book.

## Strongest cross-source findings

1. **A book concept is not a topic.** The transferable core is a defined reader and need, a governing claim or inquiry, meaningful stakes, a distinct contribution, controlled scope, and enough evidence or story progression to sustain book length. Publisher guidance treats these as an integrated proposition, not separate marketing exercises. *(Sources 1–6.)*

2. **Architecture is a dependency system.** A useful table of contents shows what each part and chapter contributes, why it appears there, what it depends on, and how it changes the reader. Reverse outlining reveals the manuscript's actual logic and exposes gaps, duplicated work, late foundations, and weak transitions. *(Sources 5–7 and 27.)*

3. **Narrative is a way of knowing, not decoration.** Scenes, cases, and people can make mechanisms, stakes, uncertainty, and change observable. Their detail must remain sourced, and their emotional force must not imply representativeness or certainty the evidence does not support. *(Sources 8–10, 17, and 18.)*

4. **Claim strength must track evidence strength.** Citation presence is not enough. Reliable nonfiction needs claim-level support, source fitness, original-source checks, quotation context, statistical interpretation, counterevidence, uncertainty, and current publication status. *(Sources 11–23.)*

5. **Clear prose preserves material qualification.** Reader-centered organization, concrete language, explicit relationships, and accessible explanation improve comprehension. Simplification becomes distortion when it removes the limitation that controls meaning. *(Sources 16, 19, 24, 25, and 28.)*

6. **Editorial stages are not interchangeable.** Concept and structural decisions precede chapter development, evidence review, line/style work, copyediting, and proofreading. Later-stage polish cannot cure a broken promise, unsupported claim, or incoherent sequence. *(Sources 26–30.)*

7. **The human author remains responsible.** AI can diagnose, organize, compare, draft controlled alternatives, and check consistency. It cannot supply lived authority, consent, accountability, verified evidence, legal judgment, or an author's substantive values and risk acceptance. *(Sources 5, 33, and 34.)*

## Professional-judgment recommendations

The following controls are synthesized editorial judgment, not rules prescribed verbatim or validated experimentally by a single source:

- Treat a concept as book-worthy when it has sustained reader value, meaningful progression, a defensible contribution, sufficient evidence or narrative material, and explicit scope. Otherwise recommend a shorter or narrower form.
- Keep a living book brief, architecture map, voice/style sheet, terminology list, claim ledger, source cards, risk/permissions notes, unresolved-issues log, decision log, and continuity log. Combine files for small projects but preserve the functions.
- Classify working claims as verified fact, attributed claim, interpretation, inference, anecdote/case, or opinion/recommendation so prose does not blur different kinds of knowing.
- Use A–D evidence confidence levels and prohibit publication-critical D claims. These labels are workflow aids, not a universal scientific grading system.
- Require approval before changing the promise, thesis, scope, chapter function/order, narrator, lived experience, conclusion, certainty, or material ethical/legal posture. Permit reversible local clarity edits within agreed authority.
- End every chapter pass with one of five statuses, updated project state, named evidence/risk gaps, author decisions, and a handoff. Recheck book-level coherence after each major part and the full manuscript.
- Use explicit quality gates and calibrated readiness stages. “Publication-ready” should mean the factual, editorial, permissions, review, version, and proof evidence exists—not that the prose sounds finished.
- Scale documentation to consequence and complexity. Low-risk explanatory prose may use a lean combined ledger; allegations, regulated advice, original research, vulnerable sources, or heavy quotation require stronger controls and professional review.

## Limitations and context dependence

- Much of the evidence is professional convention, publisher practice, or ethics guidance rather than experimental research on book outcomes. The skill therefore standardizes defensible process and traceability, not a formula for sales, acclaim, or reader response.
- Essay-writing, journalism, science, and scholarly-publishing standards were adapted only where the underlying function transfers. Narrative nonfiction, history, prescriptive trade books, and business books still need form- and field-specific judgment.
- Copyright exceptions, defamation, privacy, publicity, data protection, recording, source protection, and AI authorship rules vary by jurisdiction and facts. This appendix is not legal advice; material risk requires qualified counsel in the relevant market.
- Submission materials, platform expectations, citation style, AI disclosure, permissions practice, and editorial stage names vary by publisher, contract, genre, and route. Confirm the actual requirements.
- No authority supports a universal chapter count, word count, story ratio, three-act structure, comparable-title window, reading level, or number of sources. Use reader purpose, material, risk, and dependencies to decide.
- Sources, policies, studies, and linked pages can change. Recheck live requirements and thesis-critical research near submission or publication.
