# Intake and book diagnosis (section 3 detail)

Classify the entry state and requested operation:

| Entry state | First action |
|---|---|
| New concept | Test reader, promise, contribution, scope, evidence supply, and book-worthiness before outlining. |
| Existing outline | Reverse-outline its actual logic; test chapter jobs, dependencies, gaps, duplication, and evidence load. |
| Draft chapter | Establish its intended book-level job, adjacent dependencies, voice baseline, and claim/source state before editing. |
| Complete manuscript | Inventory the manuscript, build an actual-content reverse outline, and diagnose at book scale before local edits. |

Build or recover this minimum diagnosis:

| Field | Determine |
|---|---|
| Working title and central question | What inquiry, tension, problem, or discovery organizes the project? |
| Reader and need | Who arrives with what knowledge, desire, obstacle, or misconception? Who is not the primary reader? |
| Promise and purpose | What worthwhile change should the book produce, and why does it matter now? |
| Governing idea | What thesis, question, or narrative engine holds the book together? |
| Distinct contribution | What changes in lens, audience, evidence, method, form, or use compared with relevant books? |
| Genre and form | Argument, narrative, prescriptive, explanatory, chronological, thematic, case-led, or hybrid? |
| Scope | Period, geography, population, domains, inclusions, exclusions, and degree of generalization. |
| Voice | Author samples, narrator distance, tone, cadence, technical level, humor, and uncertainty markers. |
| Evidence | Required research, existing sources, interviews, data, archives, cases, and material gaps. |
| Risk | Living people, vulnerable subjects, confidentiality, consent, permissions, regulated advice, or contested claims. |
| Constraints | Target length, deadline, publishing route, publisher requirements, citation system, and language. |

Flag only missing inputs that block the current decision. A chapter diagnosis does not require a complete marketing plan; a legal-risk passage may require jurisdiction and source agreements.

Maintain a living project state:

- book brief and approved assumptions;
- architecture map or annotated table of contents;
- style sheet and voice profile;
- terminology, entity, chronology, and cross-reference list as needed;
- claim ledger and source cards;
- permissions, consent, confidentiality, and risk notes;
- decision log and unresolved-issues log; and
- continuity log with the current version and chapter handoffs.

Small projects may combine these records, but must retain their functions.
