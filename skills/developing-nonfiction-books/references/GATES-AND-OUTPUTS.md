# Output formats, quality gates, ethics, completion standard (sections 9-12 detail)

## 9. Output formats

Use the compact forms in [TEMPLATES.md](TEMPLATES.md) where available.

| Output | Minimum contents |
|---|---|
| Book concept brief | Reader, need, promise, governing idea, contribution, scope, method, authority, risk, constraints, and gate status. |
| Reader and positioning brief | Primary reader, situation, desired change, alternatives/comparables, material distinction, and promise test. |
| Annotated table of contents | Part and chapter jobs, reader questions, central moves, evidence/story load, dependencies, payoff, and status. |
| Chapter brief | Contract, pattern, evidence slots, counterpressure, voice notes, risks, ending, and approval needs. |
| Research plan | Questions, evidence types, likely sources, countersearch, time sensitivity, owner, stop condition, and gaps. |
| Claim ledger and source card | Exact claim-to-source trace, support level, limitations, risk, and verification status. |
| Developmental edit memo | Diagnosis by impact, evidence, options, recommendation, author decisions, and staged plan. |
| Chapter revision plan | Chapter status, contract test, prioritized changes, protected elements, fact checks, and exit criteria. |
| Line-edit sample | Original, revision, category, concise reason, voice/meaning effect, and any author choice. |
| Fact-check and citation report | Checked claims, findings, corrections, unresolved items, permissions/risk flags, and overall status. |
| Whole-manuscript readiness report | Gate-by-gate evidence, blockers, residual risk, required reviewers, and next version. |

Begin every substantive delivery with the outcome and stage status. Provide the requested artifact, then concise decision rationale, unresolved evidence/risk, updates to living state, and the next approval gate. Do not expose hidden reasoning; provide evidence, decision factors, alternatives, and risks.

## 10. Quality gates

| Gate | Pass evidence | Do not pass when |
|---|---|---|
| Concept approval | Named reader and need; defensible promise, contribution, scope, evidence/story supply, author fit, and book-length progression. | The project is only a topic, relies on vague originality, or lacks material to sustain the promise. |
| Outline approval | Every part/chapter has a distinct job; sequence, dependencies, balance, gaps, and repetition have been tested. | Order is habitual, chapters duplicate work, or central claims lack a planned home. |
| Research sufficiency | Central claims have appropriate sources; counterevidence, limitations, freshness, access, and review needs are mapped. | Thesis-critical claims are unsupported or the plan depends on evidence that cannot be obtained. |
| Draft completion | Every planned unit exists, fulfills a provisional job, and exposes rather than hides source and decision gaps. | Missing sections are disguised by transitions, placeholders are unlabeled, or the ending does not deliver the promise. |
| Chapter approval | Chapter contract is met, whole-book fit checked, status assigned, living state updated, and stage-specific blockers closed. | A material author choice, structural failure, or unverified central claim remains. |
| Manuscript-level revision | Reverse outline, argument, narrative, pacing, continuity, terminology, balance, setup/payoff, and conclusion pass as a whole. | Local improvements have created contradictions, repetition, missing foundations, or a changed promise. |
| Fact-checking and citations | A/B support for publication-critical claims; C items resolved/qualified; no D items; quotes/numbers/cases checked; source status and permissions/risk reviewed. | A citation does not support its sentence, a central source is stale/unread, or a risky claim lacks escalation. |
| Final readiness | Copyedit and proof complete; queries, cross-references, notes, links, figures, permissions, disclosures, front/back matter, versions, and required reviews closed. | Any major factual, structural, legal, ethical, permissions, production, or author-decision issue remains. |

Record the evidence used to pass each gate, the approver, date, version, residual risk, and any conditional follow-up. “Polished,” “strong,” or “publication-ready” is not a gate result without this record.

## 11. Safety, ethics, and professional boundaries

- Never fabricate or help disguise fabricated evidence, citations, quotations, interviews, cases, scenes, data, or experience. Refuse that part and offer placeholders, research steps, narrower language, or a clearly fictional exercise.
- Never hide uncertainty, convert attributed allegation into fact, imply causation from mere association, or let a vivid anecdote stand for a population.
- Preserve quotation and paraphrase boundaries. Attribution does not by itself resolve copyright; track licenses, releases, and permission needs.
- Do not assume a fixed word count or percentage makes quotation fair use or fair dealing. Flag substantial or high-value protected material for permissions or jurisdiction-appropriate review.
- Flag passages involving living or identifiable people, allegations, private facts, minors, vulnerable subjects, confidential sources, unclear consent, anonymization, or altered chronology. Seek a response where appropriate. Do not pronounce them legally safe.
- Route high-stakes medical, legal, financial, safety, statistical, historical, scientific, cultural, translation, and sensitivity questions to qualified current review when material.
- Do not imitate a living author's distinctive style. Help the author articulate their own voice or use broad traits such as concise, reflective, technical, or conversational.
- Do not manufacture first-person authority, identity, emotion, access, credentials, interviews, or lived experience.
- Protect confidential and unpublished materials. Do not place them in an unapproved system; follow consent, source, contract, security, and retention restrictions.
- Do not launder plagiarism by superficial paraphrase. Preserve credit, locators, quotation marks, and the difference between synthesis and copying.
- Treat AI as an editorial tool, not a factual source or accountable author. Independently verify external claims, preserve human decisions, and follow applicable publisher, contract, registration, and disclosure rules.
- Use targeted escalation, not generic disclaimers on every page. Name the risky passage, why it matters, what is known, and the appropriate reviewer.

Common red flags include “research shows” without an inspected source; a citation supporting a weaker claim than the sentence; untraceable quotations; statistics without denominator/date/population; composites presented as real; reconstructed scenes without disclosure; identical chapter formulas; a late shift in thesis or narrator; line polish before structural resolution; and a readiness claim without gate evidence.

## 12. Completion standard

Use calibrated stage labels:

| Stage | Good enough to proceed | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Concept ready for outlining | Book-worthy reader value, governing idea, contribution, scope, material supply, and risks are coherent. | Approved concept brief, positioning check, assumptions, exclusions, and named uncertainties. |
| Outline ready for research/drafting | The sequence produces a defensible reader progression and every chapter has a unique contract. | Annotated TOC, dependency/continuity map, evidence plan, gap/duplication check, and approval record. |
| Research ready for drafting | Enough reliable material exists to draft without manufacturing certainty; remaining gaps are explicit. | Source cards, claim ledger, counterevidence, confidence levels, risk/permissions notes, and research stop decision. |
| Chapter ready for the next stage | The chapter passes its current review mode and fits the whole book. | Chapter status, revision memo, changed claim checks, updated living state, and resolved material decisions. |
| Full draft ready for developmental editing | All intended units exist and the manuscript exposes its unresolved problems honestly. | Complete version, actual-content reverse outline, open-issues list, and no hidden missing sections or invented support. |
| Ready for professional editorial | The author has made core book decisions and supplied a stable, reviewable manuscript and source system. | Stable version; brief/outline; source and claim records; known risks; permission status; style/voice notes; declared open questions. This does **not** mean publication-ready. |
| Ready for publication preparation | Developmental, structural, chapter, evidence, line, and copy stages are closed; professional reviews required by the project are complete. | Gate record, fact-check report, citations, permissions/releases, legal or specialist sign-offs where needed, disclosures, style sheet, and resolved queries. |
| Ready for self-publishing upload preparation | Publication preparation is complete and the authoritative text can enter formatting and platform QA. | Final source file, proofed front/back matter, rights and disclosure record, metadata supplied by the author, version lock, format/accessibility checks, and proof plan. |
| Ready to publish or upload | Final formatted outputs match the authoritative text and no material blocker remains. | Final proof approval, working links/cross-references, correct figures/tables/notes, platform or printer checks, archived source/version, and author sign-off. |

If evidence is incomplete, report the highest stage actually passed and the shortest safe path to the next one. Never use “final,” “verified,” “ready,” or “complete” to mean only that prose was generated.
