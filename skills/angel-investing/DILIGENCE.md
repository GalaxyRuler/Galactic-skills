# Diligence — Tier 3 Validation

The founder interview produced claims and hypotheses. Diligence tests them. Enter the data room only after Tier 2 justifies the time, and validate the things that drive the decision *first* — don't burn three weeks confirming low-stakes details while the deal-killer sits unexamined.

---

## Staged validation

### Stage 1 — validate the decision-drivers first

The handful of facts that, if false, end the deal:

- **Team references** — previous managers, employees, co-founders, prior investors.
- **Customer / revenue** — customer calls, signed contracts, invoices, payment-processor exports, bank deposits.
- **Product** — test it yourself; walk the onboarding; look at real usage analytics.
- **Unit economics** — CAC, LTV, payback, retention, gross margin (as far as the stage supports).
- **Cap table** — founder ownership, ESOP, SAFEs/KISS/notes/warrants, side letters.
- **Regulatory feasibility** — if the sector is regulated, confirm the path exists before anything else.

### Stage 2 — standard diligence

**Product & tech** — use the product; external technical/architecture review; security/privacy review if applicable; IP filings and dates.
**Market** — talk to existing customers, prospects, and industry experts; validate the competitive slide; validate pricing and the procurement process.
**Team** — background and reference checks; reputation; prior startup outcomes; patents/publications if relevant.
**Finance** — financial-model review; bank statements; revenue reconciliation; debts and liabilities; previous financing documents; burn/runway and hiring plan.
**Cap table** — system access or step-by-step reconstruction; review all convertibles, warrants, promised options; confirm fully-diluted ownership; validate ESOP availability; model pro forma after the round.
**Legal / accounting / tax** — outsource unless the investor or team is genuinely qualified.

### Stage 3 — lower-risk / time-available

Additional customer and founder references; deeper market sizing; expanded security review; insurance/HR/vendor/operational policies; long-form model scenario analysis.

---

## Evidence hierarchy

Weight evidence by how hard it is to fake — strongest to weakest:

1. Signed customer contracts · audited or verified financials · bank statements · cap-table system access · executed legal documents.
2. Product analytics exports · payment-processor data · CRM records · cohort data.
3. Customer references and expert calls.
4. Founder-provided financial model and pitch deck.
5. Founder verbal claims.
6. Analyst assumptions.

When two sources disagree, the higher tier wins. A deck number (tier 4) never overrides a bank statement (tier 1).

---

## Evidence labeling system

Tag every datum in every output. Never let a label drift upward without new evidence.

| Label | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Verified Fact** | Backed by primary documentation — bank access, QuickBooks/ledger export, incorporation charter. |
| **Founder Claim** | A statement, metric, or projection with no supporting document yet. |
| **Customer Evidence** | From structured customer reference calls, usage logs, or verified payment history. |
| **Market Evidence** | Independent data — industry databases, expert interviews, bottom-up contract math. |
| **Financial Assumption** | A forward-looking projection, hire timeline, or spend-growth input in the model. |
| **Legal / Term Issue** | A contract term, IP gap, or regulatory hurdle requiring professional legal review. |
| **Investor Inference** | A conclusion the analyst drew from the evidence — clearly the analyst's, not the founder's. |
| **Unresolved Risk** | A material vulnerability with no mitigation plan or missing documentation. |

## Decision rules for common anomalies

When the data room throws a curveball, the move is a concrete next step or a risk frame — **not** a snap go/no-go.

| Anomaly | Next step |
|---------|-----------|
| Weak founder evidence | Track shipping velocity over a 30-60 day window; run deeper background checks. |
| Unclear customer pain | Prioritize independent customer reference calls; review unvarnished engagement logs. |
| No paying customers | Calibrate the bar down to MVP stage; focus diligence on technical execution and delivery speed. |
| Large but inaccessible market | Rebuild the market bottom-up via SAM/SOM; evaluate the immediate beachhead. |
| Weak customer retention | **Pause** the process until the team provides full multi-cohort usage data — this is a PMF risk. |
| Unclear GTM | Request CAC broken down by channel; review the live sales pipeline. |
| Negative / unknown unit economics | Model the path to margin efficiency at scale; focus on infrastructure and onboarding costs. |
| Unrealistic entry valuation | Model alternative down-round and dilution scenarios to show the return impact. |
| Messy cap table | Request corporate-counsel review; outline restructuring steps needed before close. |
| Unfavorable deal terms | Flag the non-standard clauses; provide a prioritized renegotiation list. |
| Weak diligence access | **Pause** negotiation until ledgers, tax records, and IP agreements are opened. |
| Regulatory uncertainty | Flag the specific exposure; make professional legal review mandatory before proceeding. |
| Poor investor fit | Restate the fund mandate and show exactly where the deal deviates. |

## External-verification gaps (always flag in real deals)

This skill is process and judgment, not a live data feed. In any real deal, escalate these to external sources or qualified experts — never fill them with comfortable assumptions:

- **Current market comps** — transaction and valuation comps must be sourced fresh; do not invent them.
- **Local legal / tax / securities / regulatory rules** — jurisdiction-specific; escalate to counsel.
- **Technical audit depth** — a real security/code audit is beyond a high-level product review.
- **Full financial audit** — knowing *what* to validate is not the same as performing an audit.
- **Structured customer-reference methodology** — real calls need scripts and bias controls.
- **Sector-specific benchmarks** — CAC, LTV, retention, gross margin, payback vary by model; benchmark externally.
- **Values- or religion-based compliance frameworks** — where a deal must satisfy one, use qualified experts.
- **Advanced fund modeling** — fees, carry, recycling, LP terms, and tax need careful, reviewed assumptions.
