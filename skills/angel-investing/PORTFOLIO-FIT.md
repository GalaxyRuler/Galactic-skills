# Portfolio Construction & Investor Fit

A great company can be a bad investment *for you* — wrong check size, wrong concentration, no reserves, no edge. Individual deal quality is necessary but not sufficient. This module sizes deals inside a portfolio and tests fit against your mandate.

---

## Portfolio construction

**Power-law reality:** most early-stage deals lose money or return little; a small number of outliers drive almost all returns. Random, passive, one-at-a-time angel investing underperforms. The countermeasure is structure, decided *before* you see deals.

- **Decide the budget and horizon upfront** — total capital allocated over (typically) five years; avoid rolling, deal-by-deal decisions that drift.
- **Never invest more than you can afford to lose** — treat every position as potentially zero.
- **Treat positions as illiquid** — realistic exit timelines are **7-10 years**.
- **Diversify across 15-30 independent investments** — enough bets to catch an outlier; the math of the power law demands breadth.
- **Hold a follow-on reserve** — earmark a fixed share of capital (e.g., **~50%**) to follow on into your winners; initial checks are only half the strategy.
- **Recycle** early liquidity back into new deals to maximize deployed capital.
- **Balance sector and geography** — monitor allocation so one vertical or region doesn't quietly dominate the book.

## Investor thesis & fit

**External vs internal thesis** — the external thesis is what you tell founders and the market (sector, stage, edge); the internal thesis is the category scorecard you actually evaluate against.

**Thesis categories to define positions on:** team, market, traction, product, competition, finance, execution, technology, legal, values.

**Fit checks for each specific deal:**

- **Check-size alignment** — does the target allocation sit comfortably inside your standard check?
- **Sector / stage / geography match** — do you have the domain knowledge, the right entry point, and the regional mandate?
- **Risk-tolerance calibration** — does this deal's risk profile fit your broader allocation?
- **Strategic value-add** — can you concretely help (customer intros, hiring, the next raise), or are you passive money?
- **Concentration limits** — would this over-weight a single vertical or theme?
- **Follow-on capacity** — do you have reserve room to defend ownership in later rounds?

## Deal flow

Returns start with what you get to see. Proprietary or advantaged deal flow beats inbound spray.

- Build **repeatable sourcing channels** rather than waiting for referrals.
- **Track channel quality** — which sources produce the deals you actually fund.
- Articulate your **founder-facing value proposition** — why a sought-after founder takes *your* check over an identical one.

## Syndicates & groups

Angel groups, syndicates, and SPVs change the operating model:

- **Benefits** — broader deal flow, shared diligence load, smaller individual checks, access to deals you couldn't source alone.
- **Costs** — less control, reduced direct founder access, and SPV/admin fees and overhead.
- **Choosing one** — weigh lead-investor quality, thesis match, fee structure, and SPV administration. A weak lead's diligence is not a substitute for your own on the deal-drivers.

## Post-investment support

After the wire, help the company scale without getting in its way.

- **Guide toward liquidity** — help hit the milestones that unlock the next institutional round or an exit.
- **Set a reporting cadence** — monthly or quarterly updates, agreed up front.
- **Make strategic introductions** — target customers, distribution partners, future co-investors.
- **Help with key hiring** — use your network for critical engineering/commercial leadership.
- **Advise on board and governance** — oversight, option allocation, compliance practices.
- **Avoid operational interference** — stay high-level and strategic; do not micromanage daily execution or create reporting burden that slows the team.
