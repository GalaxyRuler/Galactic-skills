# Screening — Tier 1, CSF Design, Tier 2

Covers the first two tiers of the funnel: the 5-minute screen, the Critical Success Factor design step that sits between them, and the founder interview. Tier 3 (diligence) is in [DILIGENCE.md](DILIGENCE.md).

---

## Tier 1 — the 5-minute screen

**Purpose: screen for rejection, not investment.** The only question is "does this deserve more of my time?" Reject roughly 90% here so the saved hours go to the top candidates.

Check, fast:

- **Thesis fit** — stage, sector, geography, check size, target ownership, values.
- **Team / product / market reasonableness** — does the basic shape hold together?
- **Evidence of pain + customer specificity** — a named customer with a real, urgent problem, not "everyone".
- **The three "why"s** — why this team, why now, why this product/approach.
- **Minimum metrics for the stage** — calibrated to the stage table in [SKILL.md](SKILL.md), not an absolute bar.
- **Obvious deal-breakers** — incompatible jurisdiction; broken/unfundable cap table; no runway; impossible claims; undisclosed prior investor rights; legal/regulatory impossibility; no product where one should already exist.

**Output exactly one:**

- **Reject / pass politely** — clear thesis miss or a deal-breaker.
- **Request clarification** — one or two missing facts stand between you and a decision.
- **Proceed to founder interview** — enough signal to spend 45-60 minutes.

## CSF design (do this *before* the interview)

Generic rubrics miss what actually drives a specific deal. Define **3-7 Critical Success Factors** — the few things that must go right for this company, at this stage, in this market.

For each CSF, record:

| Field | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Importance** | 1-10 — how decisive is this factor for *this* business? |
| **Evidence quality** | low / medium / high — how well-supported is it today? |
| **Test question** | the single question that most exposes real skill here |
| **Validating evidence** | what artifact would actually prove it in diligence |

Example CSFs to draw from (pick the ones that fit, don't use all):

- Team has unusual domain or customer access.
- Product has a defensible technical or data advantage.
- Customer pain is urgent *and* budgeted (a painkiller, not a vitamin).
- A go-to-market channel can scale economically (CAC/payback hold).
- Retention supports the model (the thing compounds, not leaks).
- The regulatory pathway is feasible.
- Capital required fits a venture-scale return.

CSFs feed the rubric and the override logic in [SCORING.md](SCORING.md): a high-importance CSF the company is *weak* in is a potential critical flaw; one it is exceptional in is a superpower.

## Tier 2 — the founder interview (45-60 min)

**Goals:** score skill in each CSF; capture the exact claims you will validate later; separate red and green flags; ask follow-ups until answers are specific and evidence-based; evaluate *how* founders think, not only what they say; control the conversation while staying respectful.

**Time allocation:**

| Minutes | Focus |
|---------|-------|
| 0-5 | Founder story + the problem |
| 5-15 | Team / why you |
| 15-25 | Product / market / competition |
| 25-35 | Traction / GTM / unit economics |
| 35-42 | Financials / the ask / cap table |
| 42-45 | Next steps + data-room request |

**After the interview:** score each CSF, write down every claim that needs validating, and decide — Pass, request the data room, or schedule deeper diligence.

**Interview discipline:** dig past the marketing answer; when a founder gives a headline, ask for the mechanism and the number behind it; note where they say "I don't know" and follow up (a green flag) versus where they bluff (a red flag).

## Question bank

Select questions by CSF — do not ask all of them mechanically.

**Team**
- Have you worked together before, for how long, in what context?
- What unique value does each founder bring? Why is this team better positioned than any other?
- What have you struggled with, and how did you respond?
- What skills are missing, and which role is most critical to hire next?
- How do you resolve a serious co-founder disagreement?
- What would your references say is your biggest weakness?

**Problem & customer**
- Who is your exact customer? Who pays, who uses, who decides?
- What pain are you solving, and how do you know it's urgent?
- What is the current workaround, and why is it inadequate?
- Why hasn't this been solved already? Why solve it now?

**Product & technology**
- One-sentence elevator pitch. Can customers use or buy it today?
- What single feature most drives adoption or purchase? How much better are you than the nearest competitor on it?
- What technology choices did you weigh, and who on the team is expert in this stack?
- What parts of the product are hardest to copy?
- What product risk keeps you up at night?

**Market & competition**
- How large is the market you can serve *now* vs the broader vision market?
- Who are your competitors, whose lunch do you eat first, and where are they stronger than you?
- How well-funded are competitors? Which big player could crush or acquire you?
- What do you see in this market that others miss?

**Traction & growth**
- What acquisition method works today? Walk the funnel: spend → conversion → activation → retention → revenue → referral.
- What is monthly growth, and is it sustainable? Retention/churn by cohort?
- What's the single metric that matters most right now? Your strongest proof of product-market fit?
- Which channel has failed?

**Business model & unit economics**
- How do you charge (subscription, usage, transaction, take rate, services)? What is ARPU/ACV and gross margin?
- What is CAC including salaries/overhead, and what is payback period?
- What is LTV, how did you calculate it, and what is LTV:CAC?
- What must be true for this model to scale? What breaks if growth accelerates?

**Financials & financing**
- Cash in bank, monthly burn, runway remaining?
- What are you raising, why that amount, and what milestones does it unlock?
- When is the next round, and can you raise it? Why not bootstrap?
- Any undisclosed debts, personal/family loans, or side obligations?

**Cap table & prior financing**
- Walk through the cap table. What do founders own fully diluted?
- What option pool exists and how much is available?
- What SAFEs / KISS / notes / warrants / promised options are outstanding?
- What special rights do prior investors hold? Any side letters? Who pays for the proposed ESOP increase?
- Do any rights affect future fundraising or an acquisition? Can we access the cap-table system?

**Legal / regulatory / IP**
- Where are you incorporated, and can we invest in that entity?
- What approvals, licenses, KYC, privacy, or compliance requirements apply?
- Who owns the IP — have all employees and contractors assigned it?
- What single legal issue could block scaling or acquisition?

**The art part** (your private read, asked of yourself)
- Do I trust these founders? Are values aligned?
- Do they answer directly and specifically, or generically and overconfidently?
- Do they understand what could kill the business?
- Would I want to help them for the next 7-10 years?

## Red / green-flag library

**Team** — *Green:* complementary skills + clear accountability; direct domain/customer access; resilience under stress; knows missing skills with a hiring plan. *Red:* can't say why they're uniquely positioned; missing a critical function; never worked together with no mitigation; co-CEO/family/sole-founder risk with no governance plan; arrogance or evasiveness.

**Problem / customer** — *Green:* specific customer, urgent pain, clear budget owner, validated by usage/contracts. *Red:* "everyone" is the customer; pain is generic or not urgent; can't name current alternatives; needs broad behavior change with no wedge.

**Product / tech** — *Green:* live and used; core feature materially better; team knows the trade-offs; a real moat (data/workflow/network/regulatory). *Red:* demo theater, no usage; tech chosen only because it's familiar; no defensibility in a crowded market; compliance uncertain in a regulated sector.

**Market / competition** — *Green:* clear beachhead + expansion logic; knows competitors' real strengths; credible timing catalyst; venture-scale ceiling. *Red:* TAM-only story; "no competitors"; rivals dismissed as uniformly bad; well-funded competitors with no wedge.

**Traction** — *Green:* growth *with* retention; clear revenue quality; referrals; improving cohorts; metrics match the model. *Red:* vanity metrics only; recent stall with no explanation; churn masked by new acquisition; GMV or signups presented as revenue.

**GTM / business model** — *Green:* tested channel with measured CAC/payback; knows funnel conversion and sales cycle; pricing tied to value. *Red:* "we'll spend on marketing" with no channel proof; no sales-cycle understanding; manual-only with no path to scale; unrealistic conversion assumptions.

**Financials** — *Green:* clear burn/runway; use of funds maps to milestones; realistic 18-36 month need; credible next-round narrative. *Red:* very short runway; vague use of funds; founder financial stress or undisclosed debt; raise only keeps the lights on, hits no milestone.

**Cap table & terms** — *Green:* founders still incentivized; adequate ESOP; prior rights clearly documented; terms fundable next round. *Red:* inaccurate/poorly-maintained cap table; founders over-diluted early; no option pool despite hiring needs; prior investors hold control/punitive rights; conversion or capitalization math unclear.

**Legal / regulatory** — *Green:* clean incorporation and IP ownership; understood regulatory path; complete signed docs; privacy/KYC addressed. *Red:* unknown jurisdiction or investability; regulatory issue ignored in fintech/healthcare/energy; IP unassigned; prior docs with unreviewed special terms.

**Founder behavior in the meeting** — *Green:* specific answers; knows the metrics and their limits; comfortable saying "I don't know" then following up; evidence over bravado. *Red:* overconfidence without evidence; dodges direct questions; buzzwords instead of detail; can't name what could kill the company.
